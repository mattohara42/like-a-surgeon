# M5 gate: notes for the thread test

The gate in `BUILD_PLAN.md` is that a cold reader can complete a thread
start to finish and explain what they learned. This page is for Matt, to
read before that session, as `docs/m3-gate-notes.md` was for M3.

## Set-up

Use the live site on a laptop at about 1280x800. Clear the site's stored
settings first (or use a private window), so the reader gets the first
visit: the welcome card opens by itself, with no thread finished and no
lens chosen. Sound on, volume where it starts.

## The one instruction

"Pick one of the threads on this card and follow it to the end." Nothing
else. Don't say which thread, what Next does, or what the lit lines on the
map are. Every question the reader has to ask is a finding.

There are six threads, and they are different lengths and styles:

| Thread | Stops | Years | Has a demo stop |
|---|---|---|---|
| Breaks | 5 | 1973 to 1994 | yes, the Amen break (loop or chop) |
| The Delay Line | 6 | 1968 to 1995 | yes, King Tubby (the tape echo) |
| Loud Guitars | 6 | 1964 to 1979 | yes, the first stop (clean, fuzz, slashed) |
| Songs Everyone Knew (Weird Al) | 6 | 1984 to 1996 | no |
| The Machine Nobody Wanted | 4 | 1980 to 1987 | yes, every stop (808, then 303) |
| Trains to the Bronx | 5 | 1981 to 1983 | yes, 'Planet Rock' (808 pads; the Kraftwerk stop shows the melody demo as planned, not playable) |

Which one the reader picks, and why, is worth writing down.

## What to watch for

- **Does the reader read the framing?** It is the gold italic text above
  each stop, two or three sentences. The record's own panel is below it.
  If the reader skips straight to the panel, the framing is either too
  easy to miss or not worth reading.
- **Does the reader look at the map between stops?** The camera flies to
  each stop, the timeline moves to that stop's year, and the route drawn
  so far stays lit. If nobody looks up from the drawer, those are not
  doing their job.
- **Does the reader press play on a demo stop?** Demos never start on
  their own (Q35). Note whether they were found and pressed.
- **Does the reader wander off?** Following a link out of a stop is fine
  (Q34). Note whether they find the "↩" chip at the top to come back, or
  whether they get lost.
- **Does the reader reach the outro?** The last page says what the thread
  was about and lists the other threads. Finishing ticks the thread on
  the welcome card.

## The question afterwards

"What was that thread about?" A passing answer names the idea, not only
the names. For example, for Breaks: that a few seconds of drums were
repeated, then looped, then sampled, then chopped, into new kinds of
music. For the Machine Nobody Wanted: that failing in the shops made the
machines cheap, and cheap machines reached people who used them
differently. If the answer is a list of artists, the framing has not
carried the idea.

## Also worth trying, briefly

- **Six Degrees of Weird Al.** It is on the welcome card beside the
  threads (A340), and Matt chose to leave it there for this session. If
  the reader picks it instead of a thread, write that down and let them
  play one round, then ask them to pick a thread. Whether they find the
  connection buttons, read the link labels, and reach him, or ask for
  "Show me a route", are all worth noting.

- The **Lens** row, top left. Choosing one opens a short card and dims the
  map to that kind of connection. Politics lights only 14 edges, and its
  card says so.
- **Pressing play** on the timeline. While time moves, the past more than
  twenty years back goes under a veil, and it lifts when the cursor stops.

## Known limits, so they are not mistaken for bugs

- Loud Guitars stops at post-punk. SPEC runs it to OK Go, who are not on
  the map yet.
- Trains to the Bronx steps back from 1983 to 1981 at stop 4, and its
  framing says so, because the Belleville Three edges are dated earlier.
- Only Chromium has been tested (Q27). Safari and Firefox are still to be
  checked by hand.
